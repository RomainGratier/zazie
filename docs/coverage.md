# Research coverage and evidence boundaries

Coverage revision: `0.1.0-draft.1`. Source check: **21 September 2026**.

Zazie's twelve hypotheses are a draft research pack. They ask narrow questions about submitted material. They do not determine whether a system is high-risk, certify compliance, or exhaust the AI Act. The relationships below are **project interpretations** linking observable concerns to areas worth investigating. They are not legal tests stated in the legislation.

The table is a broad inventory of the high-risk framework and adjacent obligations. A row naming a hypothesis means that it may contribute a small piece of evidence, not that the requirement is covered. A complete, paragraph-level, legally reviewed inventory remains a release gate. No such review or model-accuracy validation is claimed for this version.

## Evidence categories

These categories describe research needs, not extra API parameters. Users still supply only `intention` and `data`.

| Code | Evidence category                               | What it permits                                                                                                                                |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| E    | Event-assessable                                | A narrow observation from the submitted intention and material, when sufficient.                                                               |
| R    | Needs references or documents                   | Source records, a rubric, a procedure, a specification, or governance documents must be supplied. Their authenticity remains unverified.       |
| D    | Needs datasets or outcomes                      | Representative samples and quantitative analysis are required. A semantic classifier cannot establish population performance or fairness.      |
| O    | Needs organizational or specialist verification | People, implementation, legal interpretation, physical safety, certification, or actual organizational practice must be checked outside Zazie. |

## Adjacent obligations and deliberate exclusions

[Article 5 prohibitions](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5) require their own legal and factual analysis. The fixed pack is not an exhaustive prohibited-practices detector. In particular, matching the supplied intention never establishes that the intention itself is permissible. Article 4 literacy, [Article 50 transparency](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50), general-purpose model duties in Articles 51–56, innovation and real-world testing arrangements in Articles 57–63, and governance/enforcement provisions may apply independently of a high-risk classification. They require R/O and, where relevant, D evidence; this pack does not assess their satisfaction. The omnibus affects several of these areas.

Images, audio, video, dataset-level discrimination, hidden model behavior, mathematical correctness, actual physical state, clinical validity, identity verification, consent, and contractual rights are outside this semantic event evaluator. A textual representation of an image is assessable only as supplied text. A person is never classified as inherently risky because their record includes sensitive information.

## Gate for stronger coverage claims

Before calling any future pack reviewed or supported, publish a paragraph-level requirement register with applicability, legal version, risk, evidence category, hypothesis or explicit exclusion, review owner, and unresolved questions. Reconcile the 2026 amendments first. Have a qualified legal reviewer check the register and domain specialists check testable questions and boundary cases. Separately complete the accuracy-validation gates in [research.md](research.md). Legal mapping quality and model accuracy are different claims requiring different evidence.
